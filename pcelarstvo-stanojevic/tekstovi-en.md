## Home

### Welcome!

Bee products – honey, pollen, royal jelly and propolis – are at the very top of natural, healthy food.
Bees have given us an ideal food that meets all the needs of the human body. By combining honey,
pollen, royal jelly and propolis as a dietary supplement, we get valuable nutrients that provide
energy: building and protective substances, vitamins, minerals, enzymes, hormones and other
bioactive compounds.

### About us

Stanojević Beekeeping is a family apiary run by Goran Stanojević. Besides honey and other bee
products, we collect bee venom, build our own award-winning Apitox venom collectors, make cosmetics
based on bee venom and propolis, and run an api-chamber where visitors can breathe the air from
the hive.

ApiFigura – anti-wrinkle – the only cream with bee venom on our market that also contains
freeze-dried royal jelly. Besides tightening your wrinkles, it will leave your face soft and
nourished. Warmly recommended for people with problem skin, such as acne.

**Ingredients:** bee venom, virgin argan oil, virgin olive oil, castor oil, shea butter,

### ApiSalus – Bee Health

ApiSalus – bee health – thanks to capsaicin, one of the cream's ingredients, apitoxin (bee venom)
quickly penetrates the skin and improves circulation and blood flow in the peripheral parts of the
body. The most important components of apitoxin are melittin and adolapin. Melittin plays a role in
lowering blood pressure, prevents blood clotting, has an antibacterial effect, protects against
radiation and acts on the central nervous system. Adolapin helps relieve pain and has an
anti-rheumatic effect.

**Ingredients:** bee venom, virgin olive oil, virgin hemp oil, beeswax, manuka honey, distilled
water, eucalyptus essential oil, rosemary essential oil, chili pepper extract and vitamin E.

**How to use:** apply and rub into the target area. Repeat 2–3 times a day.

**Note:** people allergic to bee venom must not use the cream without consulting a doctor.

### Our story

Ever since he started keeping bees, Goran Stanojević has never kept them "just for honey". From the
very beginning he understood that beekeeping pays off only if you also collect pollen and propolis,
raise swarms for sale… Add the forage conditions that get worse and less predictable every year,
and this way of thinking becomes all the more important.

Goran thought differently from other beekeepers when it came to bee venom, too. Many asked him how
he could count on selling bee venom when our country has no regulations for this product, and
therefore no organised buying and no market for it at all. His answer was that the key is to change
the way we think about the market – to look at how Western markets work and to go out and find
buyers with a finished product.

It makes sense: stored properly, bee venom keeps for several years without losing quality. We believe
that, with our effort, things will change here too.

### How it works

The electrostimulator produces a pulsing electrical signal of a set frequency and amplitude
(pulse frequency 50–1000 Hz, voltage 11–42 V), grouped into bursts of a set length (2–3 second
pulses) separated by pauses with no signal (3–6 seconds). Microchips on the electrostimulator board
make it possible to change these parameters.

In most designs the collector frame is placed outside the hive, at the entrance, and can come in
different sizes. When bees land on the plate, they touch the wire grid and close the circuit, which
stimulates them. They bend their abdomen towards the plate and, as the sting touches it, release
venom onto it.

### Technology

Technology in every field grows more advanced and complex every day, and venom collection is no
exception. The basic principle, however, has stayed the same: we obtain bee venom by
electrostimulation, that is, by mild electric pulses. It is not the only way to collect venom, but it
is by far the most efficient and the simplest. It is also the least harmful – almost harmless – to
the health, quality and strength of the colony. Of course, good beekeeping practice must be followed
when collecting venom as well; then there are no negative consequences for the colonies or for
individual bees.

The devices available on the market are made in small series by individuals, mostly beekeepers,
or manufactured in China. At first glance they are all similar and repeat a similar patent from the
mid-1970s. Most consist of a power source (car battery or batteries), an electrostimulator
(controlling current, voltage, frequency…) and collector frames (with a wire grid and glass plates).

Beyond their different designs, venom collectors also differ greatly in their electronic parameters.
The biggest differences are in the control system (manual or automatic), in how the frequency and
amplitude of the output signal are controlled, and in how long the device runs. They also differ in
auxiliary features – light and sound indicators for operation and for individual stages.

### Our collector

It took Goran a great deal of time and effort to build his own bee venom collector. He drew on
information from the internet and the literature, and on his knowledge of electronics, which is his
profession. The goal was to find the optimal electronic parameters, and the only way to do that was
to build several models and test each one. After many trials and failures, in 2013 Goran arrived at
the device we present here, which has since been refined and upgraded with experience.

The device runs on 4.5 V batteries housed in the electrostimulator casing – far more practical than
a car battery. Many days in the workshop and at the apiary produced the best solution: the electronic
components built into the electrostimulator allow it to work with an alternating voltage of 23–30 V
and an oscillator frequency of 700–1000 Hz, with 3-second pulses and equally long pauses. These
settings have proven the best in practice.

There was no doubt about the wire on the collector plate. Stainless steel wire was rejected after the
first tests, because silver-plated wire performed better: it conducts better, resists the chemical
action of the venom, and silver is antiseptic (less chance of spreading disease in the apiary).
The spacing between the wires is 4.2 mm, which testing showed to be optimal.

The glass on the collector plate is 3 mm thick and coated underneath with coloured film, so the
venom is easier to see when scraping.

### Collecting technique

We start collecting bee venom in the second half of April, or slightly earlier, but only once the
colonies are strong enough – 6 frames of brood in DB hives, or 8 frames of brood in LR hives.

It all starts by placing the collector plates at the hive entrances. The plate is 15 mm thick and
360 mm long, so the entrance reducer can be removed and the plate put in its place (fixed at the
sides with wooden wedges). On bottom boards where the entrance reducer is fixed, the plates are held
with plastic clips. The difference between the inside width of the hive (372 mm) and the plate
(360 mm) leaves 6 mm on each side so drones can pass freely.

The device is programmed to switch off by itself after 45 minutes; sometimes we switch it off
earlier if we see fewer bees on the collector. We then move the plates to other hives, and only after
the second or third "treatment" do we change the glass plates and continue with another one.

The best results come in the morning, when bees are most active. During the day we do 4 "treatments"
on 10 hives at a time. On average we collect between 5 and 12 grams of venom a day this way. Per hive,
that is, per treatment of one hive, we collect 0.1 g to 0.3 g, depending on the strength of the colony.

## Api-chamber

### Api-chamber – health from the hive

The aromatic vapours of propolis, honey, wax, royal jelly and bee bread, together with the scents of
flowers, nectar and wax released from a hive full of bees, fill the air around the apiary. Many people
find time spent in this environment relaxing and refreshing – they sleep better and their appetite
and mood improve. Api-chamber sessions are a newer branch of apitherapy, a traditional practice that
uses bee products.

The api-chamber is a small wooden house used for breathing air from the hive or for lying above the
hives, with no direct contact with the bees. Both are offered from April to mid-August, when the bees
are active. The hives used for this must hold strong, healthy colonies, and they are not treated
against varroa with chemicals while sessions are running. We fight varroa only by natural means –
with a drone comb frame, and later with organic acids.

### Breathing hive air

Hive air is inhaled through special masks connected to a fan placed over an opening in the hive lid.
A dial controls how much air flows from the hive. The masks have a one-way valve so that exhaled air
does not go back into the hive.

Many visitors use these sessions to strengthen their general resilience – athletes, children and older
people – and they are especially popular with people who have trouble with their airways (nose,
sinuses, throat, bronchi).

A session lasts 10 to 20 minutes, once or twice a day; the number and schedule of sessions are
arranged individually.

### Lying above the hives

Lying above the hives is the second method offered in the api-chamber. You lie on your back on a
wooden lid with the hives underneath, knees bent, arms by your sides, palms down. You feel the
micro-vibrations made by the bees' wings and their work in the hive – a kind of vibration massage.
The steady hum of the bees works like a form of "meditation": it relaxes and calms. Bees in the hive
are also believed to ionise the air.

A session lasts from 30 minutes to an hour. It is especially appreciated by people under stress,
with chronic fatigue or sleep problems.

### Important notes

- If breathing becomes difficult or you feel agitated during a session, stop immediately.
- Sessions are not recommended for people with epilepsy, tuberculosis, cancer or acute infectious
  diseases, a fever, an allergy to bee products, or cystic fibrosis.
- Lying above the hives is not recommended during pregnancy; people with kidney or gall stones
  should be careful.
- Breathing hive air is not recommended for children under 3.
- People with chronic illnesses should consult their doctor before a session.
- Do not enter the api-chamber with a mobile phone, wearing perfume or deodorant, or after drinking
  alcohol, as this disturbs the bee colony.

*Time in the api-chamber is not a substitute for medical treatment prescribed by a doctor.*
